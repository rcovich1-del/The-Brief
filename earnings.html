Earnings - how to use this tab

Before a company reports, its option prices rise; the morning after, they usually drop fast (the "IV crush"), even if the stock barely moved. Option sellers can sell that extra premium before the report and keep it after - unless the stock moves more than expected. Pick a list, check the chart, check the expected move, put your strike outside it, and size small.

Educational only. Not a recommendation to buy or sell any security or option.

Based on a published options-education video, in our own words. Video: Why Earnings Season Is the Option Seller's Best Time of Year (YouTube, Oct 27, 2026).

The idea in plain words

In the weeks before a company reports, option prices go up because nobody knows what the report will say. The morning after, that uncertainty is gone and option prices drop fast (the "IV crush"), even if the stock barely moved. Option sellers can sell that extra premium before the report and keep it after. The catch: sometimes the stock moves much more than expected, and that move can wipe out the gain.

Step 1 - Pick a list

  • Top 1% Buys: Barchart's highest-rated stocks with earnings in the next 3 weeks. Start here.
  • Uptrend + Earnings: Buy-rated stocks trading above their key moving averages, with earnings coming up.
  • All Upcoming Earnings: every large company reporting in the next 35 days. Useful for browsing; skip names in downtrends or with Sell ratings.
  • Tags: "Reports before next open" means it's too late to enter before earnings. "Thin options" means low call volume, so bid/ask spreads may be wide.

Step 2 - Look at the chart

Before anything else, open the chart. Prefer stocks in clear uptrends. A high-premium stock with a weak chart isn't a good candidate.

Step 3 - Check the expected move on Market Chameleon (free)

  1. Go to marketchameleon.com and search the ticker.
  2. Open the stock's Earnings section, then Release Dates.
  3. Find the next earnings date and its Implied Straddle %. That is the expected move: how far the options market thinks the stock could move either way, priced from the at-the-money options for the first expiration after earnings.
  4. Work out the downside level: price × (1 − expected move). Example: AAPL at $347.10 with a 6.1% expected move → $347.10 × 0.939 ≈ $326.

Why Market Chameleon and not Barchart's number: Barchart's expected move uses only about 80-85% of the straddle, so it shows a smaller move (5.17% vs 6.1% for AAPL in the video). Use the larger Market Chameleon number.

Past earnings moves: Market Chameleon can also show how far the stock actually moved after past reports compared with what was expected. Some of that history may need a paid account. Stocks that usually stay inside their expected move are better candidates.

Note: the expected move changes as earnings gets closer. Check it again on the day you place the trade.

Step 4 - Choose the expiration and strike

  • Use the first expiration after the earnings report. The Time column helps: a company reporting Thursday after the close is covered by that Friday's expiration.
  • Put your short strike below the downside level from Step 3 (outside the expected move). In the AAPL example, that means the 320 or 315 put, not the 330.
  • This doesn't guarantee a win. It puts your strike past where the market expects the stock to go.

Step 5 - Pick a structure

  • Cash-secured put: only on a stock you'd be happy to own at that strike. If the stock gaps down and you're assigned, you own a stock you wanted, at a discount.
  • Bull put spread: defined risk, and needs much less capital than a cash-secured put. A gap through your strikes hurts but has a known maximum loss.
  • Iron condor: spreads on both sides of the expected move. Only for large, stable companies whose past earnings moves have been small. Avoid on fast-growing, high-volatility stocks.
  • Fills: weekly options around earnings often have wide bid/ask spreads. Work your order near the mid price. Payoff tools that assume the worst fill will understate your premium.

Step 6 - Size small

Earnings trades should be smaller than your normal trades: about 1-2% of the account per trade, versus a typical 3-5%. Before entering, look at the trade's risk graph in your broker's analysis tool and check what a gap past your strikes would cost.

Know the risks

  • Gaps: stocks can move far beyond the expected move. In early 2026 Intel's options priced about a 9% move and the stock fell 17%. A naked put takes a large loss; a bull put spread can take a full loss.
  • No time to fix it: the move happens overnight, so there's no chance to roll or adjust before the damage is done.
  • IV crush hits long options too: if your structure includes a long option (for example, a poor man's covered call), it loses value in the crush as well.

How the lists are cleaned

  • Names whose earnings date is today or earlier are removed: the lists are made after the close, so they have already reported.
  • A name reporting before the open on the next trading day is greyed out, tagged "Reports before next open" and moved to the bottom.
  • Alphabet appears once (GOOGL), not twice.
  • Days Away counts calendar days from the edition date. When: This Week and Next Week are Monday-to-Sunday weeks.
