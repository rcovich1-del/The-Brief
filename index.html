Bull put spread

What this screen looks for: stocks and ETFs priced $20 to $500, above their 50-day average, with options expensive for them (IV Rank over 40, IV Percentile over 50) and heavy trading.

What it is: Sell a put and buy a lower-strike put with the same expiration, collecting a credit. It keeps the credit if the stock stays above the sold strike at expiration.

Main risk: If the stock falls below the bought put, the loss is the gap between the two strikes minus the credit, which is usually several times the credit.

Cash-secured put

What this screen looks for: stocks and ETFs priced $20 to $200, above their 50- and 200-day averages, with options expensive for them (IV Rank over 40, IV Percentile over 50) and heavy trading.

What it is: Sell a put while keeping enough cash to buy 100 shares at the strike, collecting a premium.

Main risk: If the stock falls below the strike, the seller may have to buy 100 shares at the strike, well above the market price; the loss grows the further the stock falls, less only the premium.

Bullish diagonal

What this screen looks for: stocks and ETFs priced $20 to $500, above their 50- and 200-day averages, with options middling for them (IV Rank 20 to 40, IV Percentile 50 to 80) and heavy trading.

What it is: Buy a longer-dated call and sell a shorter-dated call at a higher strike, paying a net debit.

Main risk: A sharp drop in the stock, or a fall in implied volatility, can lose most or all of the debit paid; a sharp rise past the sold strike also cuts into the gain.
