Each indicator gets a mark - Risk On, Neutral or Risk Off - from the rules at the bottom of this section. Each index's reading is the mark it has most of; if two marks tie, the more cautious one wins. An indicator that is not available is left out of the count. When the trend marks and the breadth marks point opposite ways (at least 2 of the 3 trend marks Risk On and at least 2 of the 3 breadth marks Risk Off, or the other way round), a short tag under the reading says so: "narrow breadth" or "broad participation".

Risk-On score: each indicator's mark counts 1 for Risk On, 1/2 for Neutral and 0 for Risk Off, every indicator weighted the same; the score is the average, as a percentage (100% = every counted indicator Risk On, 0% = every one Risk Off). It sums up the marks; it is not a probability. One mark moves it about 6 points, so a 5-day average is shown too. The reading above still goes by the most marks, so near a tie the two can differ: 4 Risk On, 1 Neutral and 4 Risk Off scores 50% but reads Risk Off.

Group score: the same marks, averaged inside each group first (Trend, Momentum, Breadth, Volatility), then across the groups. The trend group has three indicators that usually move together, so the Group score gives each group one equal vote. An indicator that is not available is left out of its group. It is shown for comparison only; it is not a probability, and the reading above still goes by the most marks.

Bull put spread

What this screen looks for: stocks and ETFs priced $20 to $500, above their 50-day average, with options expensive for them (IV Rank over 40, IV Percentile over 50) and heavy trading.

What it is: Sell a put and buy a lower-strike put with the same expiration, collecting a credit. It keeps the credit if the stock stays above the sold strike at expiration.

Main risk: If the stock falls below the bought put, the loss is the gap between the two strikes minus the credit, which is usually several times the credit.
