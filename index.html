Bull put spread

What this screen looks for: stocks and ETFs priced $20 to $500, above their 50-day average, with options expensive for them (IV Rank over 40, IV Percentile over 50) and heavy trading.

What it is: Sell a put and buy a lower-strike put with the same expiration, collecting a credit. It keeps the credit if the stock stays above the sold strike at expiration.

Main risk: If the stock falls below the bought put, the loss is the gap between the two strikes minus the credit, which is usually several times the credit.

What it is: A bull put spread (sell a put, buy a lower-strike put, same expiration, for a credit) placed while an earnings report is due.

Main risk: The report can gap the stock below both strikes overnight, giving the full loss (the gap between the strikes minus the credit) with no chance to adjust first.

What this screen looks for: stocks (no ETFs) priced $20 to $500, above their 50- and 200-day averages, reporting earnings within the next 30 days, with IV Rank 30 to 40 and IV Percentile over 50, and heavy trading.

What it is: A bullish diagonal (buy a longer-dated call, sell a shorter-dated call at a higher strike, for a debit) placed around an earnings report.
