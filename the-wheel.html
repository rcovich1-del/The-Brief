The Wheel

Option contracts from Barchart's end-of-day wheel screens, for learning and comparing the wheel strategy.

  1. 1 Sell a cash-secured put
  2. 2 If assigned, you own 100 shares at your cost basis
  3. 3 Sell covered calls at or above that cost basis
  4. 4 If the shares are called away, start again

How to use this page

  • The page opens on a starting view. Move any slider, or type a number in its Min or Max box and press Enter, to widen or narrow it - every slider reaches the full range of today's data. A number outside the data is moved to the nearest allowed value, with a short note. Check earnings dates yourself. Barchart's files carry no earnings dates, and an earnings report before expiry can move a stock far past your strike.

Cash-Secured Puts

Step 1 of the wheel. You sell a put and set aside the cash to buy 100 shares at the strike if you are assigned. No shares needed.

Tinted rows marked <$5 are stocks under $5. If a low-priced stock keeps falling after you are assigned, calls above your cost basis may pay almost nothing - the one wheel problem that writing calls cannot fix.

Covered Calls

Step 3 of the wheel. A covered call needs 100 shares you already own, usually from an assigned put.

Buying shares today just to sell a call against them is a buy-write - a different trade with its own risks, not the wheel.

What the columns mean

Price, Strike, Expires, DTE
The stock's closing price, the option's strike and expiry date, and the days to expiry counted from the quote date on Barchart's file.
Money-ness
How far the strike is from the stock price, as Barchart shows it. Negative means out of the money.
Bid
The premium per share a seller could expect at the bid. One contract is 100 shares.
Cost basis (puts)
Strike minus premium: what each share would really cost you if assigned (Barchart's "BE (Bid)").
Net cost (calls)
Stock price minus premium: your cost per share for owning the stock with the call sold (Barchart's "BE (Bid)" and "Net Debit", which are the same number).
Premium return
Puts: premium divided by cost basis (Barchart's "Ptnl Rtn"). Calls: premium divided by net cost (Barchart's "Return").
Ann. return (puts)
Barchart's annualized premium return ("Ann Ptnl Rtn").
If called (calls)
Barchart's return if the shares are called away at the strike: premium plus the gain up to the strike ("Ptnl Rtn").
Ann. if not called (calc.)
Calls: the yearly rate of Barchart's "Return" if the shares are NOT called away (premium only) - worked out by this page as Return x 365 / DTE, because Barchart does not send it.
Ann. if called (Barchart)
Calls: Barchart's own "Ann Ptnl Rtn" - the yearly rate of the return if the shares ARE called away at the strike (premium plus the gain up to the strike).
Delta
Shown without its sign (Barchart shows covered-call deltas as negative). Roughly how much the option moves for a $1 move in the stock; often read as a rough chance of ending in the money.
OTM prob.
Barchart's estimate of the chance the option expires out of the money.
IV
Implied volatility: how big a move the option price is expecting, per year.
Volume, Open int.
Contracts traded today, and contracts still open. Low numbers can mean wide prices when you trade.

Barchart's "BE%" column is not shown on the put list: for these files it measures from strike plus premium, which is not a put's break-even.

Where the wheel hurts

  • A stock that keeps falling. After assignment you own shares worth less than you paid. Calls at or above your cost basis may pay almost nothing, and calls below it can lock in a loss.
  • Low-priced stocks. Under $5, a falling stock can drop so far that no call above your cost basis pays anything. That is why those rows are tinted.
  • Capped upside. A covered call caps your gain at the strike. If the stock rallies hard, the gain above the strike goes to the call buyer.
  • Earnings and news. A report or event before expiry can gap the stock straight through your strike.
  • High premium usually means high risk. A big annualized return often means the market expects a big move.
  • Cash tied up. Each put needs strike x 100 in cash until it expires or is closed.
  • Early assignment. These options can be assigned before expiry, especially around dividend dates.
